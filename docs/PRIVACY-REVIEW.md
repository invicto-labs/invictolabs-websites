# Privacy notice — launch review

The /privacy-policy page is deliberately labelled a draft. Do not remove that notice until the business owner and appropriate legal reviewer confirm the practices and disclosures below. A generated website notice is not legal certification.

- Confirm the responsible legal entity, registered address and privacy contact. The current copy uses the existing Invicto brand and marketing@invictolabs.com without inventing a legal controller.
- Confirm hosting configuration (including Vercel logs, analytics and monitoring), email provider, recruitment platform, access permissions, subprocessors and processing countries.
- Confirm purposes, applicable legal bases, data sharing, any sale/sharing definitions under relevant law, and browser privacy-signal handling. Absence of advertising code does not establish all business practices.
- Set actual retention/deletion and backup procedures for enquiries and applications; no retention duration has been invented.
- Confirm applicable individual rights, verification and response procedures, regulator/grievance contacts, and international-transfer disclosures based on visitors, business establishment and applicable law.
- Audit the deployed site's network traffic and cookies. Current source has no Google Analytics or advertising pixel integration, but hosting configuration is not established by source review alone.
- Review product-specific notices and client processing agreements separately. This page covers the marketing website, not confidential production workflows.
- Set an effective date once approved; replace provisional paragraphs and remove the draft banner only after approval. Revisit the notice when tracking, forms, hosting or product integrations change.

Verified application behaviour: the contact form prepares an in-memory email draft; sending is performed by the visitor's email app. Clipboard copying is explicit. It is not a server-submitted form.

Reference guidance consulted 29 October 2026: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/privacy-security — privacy statements must reflect actual practices.
